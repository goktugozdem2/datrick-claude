Why partners call

The entry point is usually a client consequence, not a planned vendor search.

Urgency

A key technical owner leaves or refuses a usable handover

Your client still expects continuity, but the environment, access, dependencies, runbooks, reporting, or recurring responsibilities are no longer under reliable ownership.

Capacity

A signed client need arrives before the capacity plan

The opportunity is real, but your internal team is committed, the required specialist is missing, or adding permanent capacity for one uncertain workstream would be premature.

Specialism

The work crosses data or AI domains outside the core team

DBA/NOC, migration QA, BI reliability, analytics, Claude workflows, or technical model evaluation requires judgment the account team cannot credibly improvise.

Trust

The client expects senior communication as well as execution

Scope, assumptions, status, blockers, risks, decisions, documentation, escalation, and acceptance must remain visible enough for the partner to stay in control.

Account safeguards

Protect the relationship with explicit boundaries before delivery starts.

Commercial

Your firm remains the account owner

You control client pricing, packaging, relationship decisions, business priorities, and commercial commitments. Datrick scopes and prices its delivery relationship with the partner.

Communication

Client contact follows the approved model

Datrick can work partner-only, join as a named specialist, or operate under your brand. Identity, meetings, email routes, escalation, and final client-facing approval are agreed.

Confidentiality

Account and technical information have defined use

Confidentiality, non-solicitation expectations, branding, access, retention, permitted use, and disclosure boundaries belong in written engagement terms.

Access

Production authority stays attributable

Named accounts, least privilege, secure credential handling, environment scope, change approval, expiry, revocation, and emergency procedures are explicit.

Evidence

Work leaves an operating record

Status, assumptions, decisions, changes, tests, risks, known issues, runbooks, and acceptance evidence reduce dependency on memory or informal chat.

Capacity

Staffing is confirmed, not implied

Named roles, coverage, availability, workload, minimum commitment, escalation, and substitutions require capacity review before the partner makes a client promise.

Partner FAQ

Questions to settle before presenting Datrick in a client plan.

Which IT service firms are the best fit for Datrick?

The strongest fit is an IT service firm, outsourcing provider, MSP, consultancy, or agency with an active client need, an accountable account owner, a realistic delivery budget, and authority to establish the required access and communication model. Datrick does not reserve capacity for speculative resale with no defined client demand.

Does Datrick communicate directly with the partner's client?

Only under the communication model approved by the partner. Datrick can work partner-only, join as a named specialist, or operate under the partner's brand. Identity, meeting participation, email routes, escalation, and final client-facing approval are agreed before delivery begins.

How does Datrick protect the partner's account and margin?

The partner retains the client relationship, resale pricing, commercial commitments, priorities, and final approval. Datrick prices and scopes its delivery relationship with the partner. Confidentiality, non-solicitation expectations, communication boundaries, branding, and escalation belong in the written engagement terms.

Which services can Datrick deliver for a partner's client?

Typical entry points include DBA/NOC and database operations, emergency database support, database handover recovery, migration development and QA, BI reporting reliability, analytics and pipelines, Claude workflow implementation, and technical AI model training or evaluation. Final scope depends on specialist fit, access, risk, and capacity.

How does a new partner engagement start?

Start with a written client scenario: what is happening, what is at risk, service area, environment, expected workload, urgency, access constraints, communication model, acceptance criteria, and what has already been promised. Datrick then responds with fit, qualifying questions, and the smallest responsible starting shape.
