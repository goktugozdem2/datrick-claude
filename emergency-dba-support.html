What qualifies

Bring Datrick in when database risk is affecting operations, delivery, or ownership.

Production

Availability or performance is degrading

Queries, connections, jobs, locks, replication, capacity, or dependent applications are failing or slowing enough to affect users or client commitments.

Recovery

Backup or restore confidence is unclear

The team cannot confirm successful backups, restore readiness, retention, recovery point expectations, or who is authorized to coordinate recovery.

Ownership

The responsible person is unavailable

A DBA, developer, manager, or vendor left, stopped responding, or holds essential context that was never transferred into an operating runbook.

Recurrence

The same incident keeps returning

Symptoms are repeatedly cleared without a documented cause, risk owner, monitoring change, permanent action, or follow-up review.

Delivery

A migration or client milestone is blocked

Database defects, validation gaps, access problems, performance risk, or unresolved ownership are preventing a responsible transition.

Continuity

Reporting and pipelines are fragile

Critical dashboards, refresh jobs, integrations, or data feeds depend on undocumented database behavior and cannot tolerate another failure.

First response

Urgency does not remove the need for authorization, evidence, and a controlled plan.

  1. 1

    Written intake

    Describe what is at risk, what changed, what has been tried, the timeline, available evidence, and who can authorize access and changes.

  2. 2

    Qualification

    A senior lead reviews the request, confirms whether Datrick can responsibly help, identifies missing context, and defines the immediate boundary.

  3. 3

    Controlled triage

    Begin with minimum necessary access, preserve evidence, establish a communication path, and separate observation from approved intervention.

  4. 4

    Ownership plan

    Record findings, decisions, remaining risk, next actions, and whether the work should continue as recovery, stabilization, or ongoing operations.

What to provide

Send enough context to qualify the risk without sending secrets through the form.

Incident context

Describe the environment and impact

  • Database platform, version, hosting model, and environment.
  • Business services, users, reports, or client commitments affected.
  • Symptoms, start time, error evidence, alerts, and recent changes.
  • Actions already attempted and the result of each action.
  • Backup, restore, replication, and recovery information currently available.
Authorization

Name the people who control access and decisions

  • Current technical owner and business or client escalation contact.
  • Person authorized to approve access and production changes.
  • Approved credential-transfer and communication channels.
  • Existing vendor, cloud, security, or infrastructure support paths.
  • Required status cadence and who should receive incident updates.

Do not submit passwords, private keys, access tokens, customer records, or sensitive logs through the public form. Secure access and evidence transfer are established only after written qualification.

Representative engagement pattern

A common entry point is an urgent ownership gap inside a larger client delivery relationship.

The prime provider retains the client

An IT service firm remains accountable to its professional client, but a key database owner leaves or the existing team cannot resolve an active issue. The provider needs specialist delivery without transferring the client relationship.

Boundaries

Emergency support must remain technically and commercially responsible.

Datrick can

Assess, coordinate, stabilize, and document

Datrick can review evidence, support diagnosis, coordinate approved recovery work, examine backup and restore readiness, identify ownership gaps, document risk, and establish a responsible support plan.

Datrick cannot

Guarantee recovery before inspecting the environment

Datrick cannot promise data restoration, incident resolution, a start time, or a fixed outcome before reviewing access, evidence, platform condition, third-party dependencies, approvals, and contractual scope.

Change control

No production action without authorization

Observation and evidence collection are separated from changes. Production actions require a named approver, an understood risk, an agreed communication path, and an available rollback or recovery decision where practical.

Shared responsibility

Infrastructure and vendors may remain essential

Cloud providers, hosting teams, security owners, application teams, network operators, software vendors, and client stakeholders may control dependencies Datrick cannot change independently.

FAQ

Questions technical and delivery leaders ask before urgent DBA support begins.

How quickly can Datrick start emergency DBA support?

Urgent written inquiries are prioritized. Datrick confirms availability, scope, access requirements, and a responsible start plan after reviewing the incident. The website does not guarantee a specific response or start time.

What information should we send with an urgent database request?

Describe the database platform and version, environment, business impact, incident start time, symptoms, recent changes, monitoring or error evidence, backup and restore status, current owner, available access, and required communication path. Do not send passwords or secrets through the website form.

Does emergency DBA triage require production write access?

Not always. Datrick starts with the minimum authorized access needed to understand the incident and prefers read-only evidence collection where practical. Any production change requires a named approver, an understood risk, and an agreed execution path.

Can Datrick guarantee database recovery or data restoration?

No. Recovery depends on backups, logs, infrastructure, platform behavior, access, prior changes, data condition, and third-party systems. Datrick can assess evidence, coordinate restore or recovery work, document risk, and recommend the safest responsible next action.

Can Datrick support an IT service firm under its brand?

Yes. Datrick supports prime IT service firms that retain ownership of the end-client relationship. Communication, escalation, documentation, access, and client-facing boundaries are defined during written scoping.

Can emergency DBA work become ongoing database operations support?

Yes. After the immediate risk is understood, Datrick can scope a stabilization sprint or ongoing database operations program covering monitoring, incident handling, backups, restore readiness, performance, documentation, reporting dependencies, and improvement backlog.
